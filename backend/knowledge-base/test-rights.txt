Fourth Amendment - Search and Seizure Rights

The right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, and effects, against unreasonable searches and seizures, shall not be violated.

Police must have legal justification to stop, search, or seize people or property. Police cannot search a phone or home just because they want to.

Warrant Requirement: Search warrants must be approved by a judge and supported by evidence. Police generally need a warrant to enter a home.

Right to Refuse Consent: You can refuse to consent to searches. Saying "I do not consent to any searches" preserves rights even if a search proceeds.

Miranda Rights: You have the right to remain silent and to counsel during custodial interrogation. Police must inform suspects of these rights.

Traffic Stops: Police need reasonable suspicion to stop your vehicle. You must provide license, registration, and insurance, but you can remain silent beyond that.

You can refuse consent to vehicle searches. Police need probable cause or a warrant to search without consent.

Right to Ask If Free to Leave: If you are not being detained or arrested, you can ask whether you are free to go.
